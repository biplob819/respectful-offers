# Decision log

Append-only. Each entry: ID, date, decision, alternatives, reason. `OWNER D-n` marks a ruling made by the owner. Grep this file before asking the owner a question.

| ID | Date | Decision | Alternatives considered | Why |

| OWNER D-1 | 2026-11-15 | The primary result is a multi-period **simulator rollout** of every policy on the same evaluation members under common random numbers (CRN). Inverse propensity scoring (IPS), self-normalised IPS (SNIPS) and doubly robust (DR) estimates are computed on period-1 logged decisions and **validated against the simulator's exact oracle value** (owner ruling, Q3) | OPE on simulated logs as the headline (PRD Flow A); rollouts only, no OPE | When the simulator generates both the logs and the truth, OPE can only recover the YAML (C1). Rules R1, R2 and R8 and the 90-day value are sequential, and single-step OPE cannot evaluate a policy whose allowed actions depend on its own past choices (C3). Validating OPE against a known truth turns the weakness into evidence that the estimators work, which is what a real A/B log would need |
| D-2 | 2026-11-15 | The simulator draws a hidden **response segment** per member (sure thing, persuadable, lost cause, sleeping dog) from a mix that depends on real features (risk band × listening-trend band). Base renewal is 1 − the calibrated churn risk from the real model. Actions have an exposure effect and an acceptance effect per segment | The PRD's renewal-probability table per action × risk band | With a table keyed only on risk band, the optimal policy can be read straight from the YAML and the bandit has nothing to learn (C2). Sleeping dogs are what make "no offer" the right answer for some members, so R5 means something |
| D-3 | 2026-11-15 | **Pre-registration.** The owner sets all three variants' values, `offers prereg` records the YAML's sha256 in `docs/prereg.md` and the decision log, and only then does `offers evaluate` run on real data. A changed YAML needs a new decision and a rerun of every variant. Runs with `--exploratory` are stamped and cannot be published | Set values freely on build day | Without a freeze, values can drift until the bandit wins (C6). Mirrors EvalKit's held-out rule (EvalKit §5 step 7) |
| D-4 | 2026-11-15 | The respect filter is a pure function placed in front of every respectful policy. A separate **auditor** recomputes every rule from the decision log and member history, and does not import the filter (enforced by a test) | Trust counters kept by the filter itself | A filter that counts its own violations reports zero when it is wrong (H8). Same principle as EvalKit L-10: check against an independent source |
| D-5 | 2026-11-15 | The reward is **net revenue per period**: renewal revenue after any discount. Headline outcomes are **incremental** against the no-offer policy on the same members (CRN), and cost is reported per incremental retained member | Renewal as the reward; raw retained counts; cost per retained member (PRD US2) | Optimising renewal always favours discounts (H1). Raw retained counts include members who would have stayed anyway, so deadweight discount spend disappears from the cost metric (H2) |
| D-6 | 2026-11-15 | The bandit is **disjoint Bayesian linear Thompson sampling** (one ridge posterior per action, NumPy only). It is trained in simulation on a training cohort and then **frozen**. Its action probabilities come from Monte Carlo posterior draws | Beta-Bernoulli per context cell; LinUCB; online learning during evaluation; the Open Bandit Pipeline library | The reward is continuous, the model is easy to explain to a reviewer, and a frozen stochastic policy has well-defined propensities for OPE (H5). A small in-house module keeps dependencies light and the estimators inspectable |
| D-7 | 2026-11-15 | The budget (R4) is a **per-period face-value pacer** applied after the policy chooses. Offers are admitted in priority order until the period's budget would be passed, and the rest are downgraded to the member's best free allowed action. The logging policy is an unbudgeted randomised pilot | Lagrangian or knapsack bandits; a budget counted on redemptions | Charging face value at send time guarantees spend stays within budget whatever members accept (H4). Keeping the budget out of the logger keeps its propensities exact and independent across members |
| D-8 | 2026-11-15 | Cohorts come from **salted hash buckets of the member id** over the March cohort: holdout 10%, bandit training 30%, logging pilot 20%, evaluation 40% | A random split per run | Stable across runs and machines, checkable for leakage, and compatible with CRN (H6) |
| D-9 | 2026-11-15 | The churn model trains on the **February** cohort, is calibrated by isotonic regression on a separate February fold, and is reported on **March**. Every feature is computed as of the first day of its label month. Both the churn model and the policies use an explicit **feature allow-list**, which excludes age, gender, city and registration channel | Random cross-validation; calibrate on the test month; a deny-list of age and gender | KKBox's churn label is defined by transactions after expiry, so anything from the label window leaks it (C4). Calibrating on the reporting month inflates its quality (H11). Risk feeds R2, so excluding protected attributes only from "decision features" is not enough, and city or channel can act as proxies (H7) |
| D-10 | 2026-11-15 | A lean, synchronous **stage runner with a content-addressed artifact store**. Cache keys are built from stage, code version, input artifact hashes, config hash and seed.
